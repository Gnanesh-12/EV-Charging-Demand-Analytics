# EDA visual insights (generated from the data)

Data: 31,387 sessions at 55 chargers, 2018-04-25 to 2021-09-13 (1132 days with data); normal operation (R1–R3): 27,898 sessions on 691 days. Figures are in `eda_figures/`; statistical tables and tests are in Issue #10 (`eda_tables/`, `eda_key_findings.md`).

| Figure | Topic | Insight |
|---|---|---|
| `F01_daily_sessions.png` | Daily sessions over time | In normal operation weekdays average 48 sessions vs 21 on weekends (2.4x). Weekday volume averages 74 (R1), 89 (R2), 37 (R3), 9 (R4). Paid charging changed weekday volume by -58% (R3 vs R2) and the level held until COVID; the COVID period averaged -76% relative to R3, and the last 8 weeks of data are at 61% of the R3 weekday level. Shaded: 106 days with no recorded sessions. |
| `F02_arrivals_by_hour.png` | Arrivals by hour of day | Weekday arrivals peak at 8:00 (10.2 per day); 45% of weekday arrivals fall between 07:00 and 10:00. Weekend arrivals are flatter (peak-to-mean 2.2 vs 5.1 on weekdays). The 17:00–20:00 share of weekday arrivals is 16% in R1, 19% in R2, 8% in R3 — it falls under paid charging, consistent with the dataset authors' explanation that evening arrivals are price-sensitive community (non-campus) users. |
| `F03_arrival_heatmap.png` | Arrivals by weekday and hour | The busiest cell is Wed 8:00 with 10.8 arrivals per day. Weekday peak hours are Mon 8:00, Tue 8:00, Wed 8:00, Thu 8:00, Fri 8:00 — the same morning pattern every working day; Saturday and Sunday peak at 2.1 arrivals per hour-slot. |
| `F04_occupancy_profile.png` | Plugged-in vehicles by time of day | On weekdays the number of plugged-in vehicles rises from about 3 overnight to a mean of 28 around 13:00 and stays high until mid-afternoon; the 90th percentile reaches 43 and the maximum 51 of 54 chargers. 5 of 493 weekdays reach ≥90% occupancy at some point, so capacity is tight only on the busiest days. |
| `F05_energy_histogram.png` | Energy per session | Median 6.7 kWh per session (mean 8.9): most sessions are well below a full battery. The 13–14 kWh bin holds 2,480 sessions, 2.8x its neighbours. During free charging (R1–R2), 16% of unclaimed sessions land in that bin vs 5% of claimed ones — consistent with the 14 kWh default allowance for unclaimed sessions (Issue #10, Section 7e). |
| `F06_daily_energy_and_concentration.png` | Daily energy and energy concentration | Energy delivered per day averages 577 kWh (R1), 707 kWh (R2), 260 kWh (R3), 96 kWh (R4), so daily load follows session volume (correlation 0.97). Energy is moderately concentrated: the largest 10% of sessions deliver 30% of all energy and the largest half 81%. |
| `F07_duration_histogram.png` | Session duration | Plugged-in time has two modes, short visits and full-workday stays, separated at about 5.9 h; 46% of sessions are long stays. Charging itself is much shorter (median 2.1 h vs 5.2 h plugged in), so 48% of plugged-in hours are idle — a car occupying a charger without drawing power. |
| `F08_arrival_duration_energy.png` | Arrival time, stay length and energy | Arrival time and stay length have a moderate negative relationship (Spearman rho -0.40): arrivals at 07:00–10:00 stay a median 8.1 h, arrivals at 17:00–20:00 2.1 h. Energy and stay length have a moderate relationship (rho 0.37) — energy grows far less than stay length, so long stays are mostly parking time, which is what makes them schedulable. |
| `F09_station_utilization.png` | Utilization by charger | Use is uneven but not extreme (Gini 0.34 on sessions): the busiest charger averages 2.1 sessions per day, the quietest 0.0. Occupancy ranges from 0% to 44% of the clock (median 19%). The rank correlation between sessions per day and occupancy is 0.94, so session counts are a fair guide to occupancy. |
| `F10_station_week_heatmap.png` | Sessions per charger per week | During normal operation, 8 of 55 chargers have sessions in every complete week after their first use; 47 have at least one empty week (947 charger-weeks), which more likely marks a fault or out-of-service period than zero demand — worth checking before using per-charger rates. Under paid charging (R3), 54 of 55 chargers average fewer sessions per week than in Sep–Oct 2018, so the drop is network-wide. In the COVID period 48 of 55 chargers recorded any session. |
| `F11_distributions_by_regime.png` | Distributions by regime and day type | In R1, weekend stays have a median of 2.5 h vs 5.6 h on weekdays, with 8.5 vs 7.3 kWh per session; weekend drivers stay much shorter for the same or more energy, i.e. they come to charge rather than to park for work. Under paid charging, weekday sessions have a median of 6.3 h and 6.1 kWh (R1: 5.6 h, 7.3 kWh); longer stays with less energy fit the loss of short community visits and the 30-minute cut-off for unclaimed sessions. In the COVID period weekday sessions have a median of 1.8 h and 6.4 kWh. |
| `F12_pattern_comparisons.png` | Claimed vs unclaimed, short vs long, regimes | (1) Median energy of unclaimed sessions is 7.4 kWh in R1, 7.2 kWh in R2, 0.9 kWh in R3, 0.9 kWh in R4; it collapses when the 30-minute cut-off starts, so energy for unclaimed sessions reflects the operator's rules, not drivers' need. (2) 63% of long stays arrive 07:00–10:00 vs 22% of short stays; long stays are morning commuters, short stays arrive across the day. ## Caveats for the modelling stage
- The data contains four regimes (two pricing/default changes in 2018 and the COVID period from March 2020). Models must include the regime or be evaluated within one.
- Days with data per regime: 129 (R1), 61 (R2), 501 (R3), 441 (R4).
- 106 days have no recorded sessions; they are a recording gap, not zero demand, and must be excluded.
- Energy for unclaimed sessions is shaped by default allowances, so it is not a direct measure of drivers' energy need.
- Annual seasonality cannot be separated from regime changes: the only full year in one regime is the paid year before COVID.
