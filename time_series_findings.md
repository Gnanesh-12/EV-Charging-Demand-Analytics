# Issue #16: Time-series findings

Generated by `notebooks/EV_Charging_Demand_Analysis_i16.ipynb`; every number below is computed there.

## Data and preparation

- **Series.** Daily charging sessions (primary), daily kWh and daily peak plugged-in vehicles, built from the full
  collection (31,387 sessions, 25 Apr 2018 – 13 Sep 2021) with the Issue #9 cleaning rules. On the
  Issue #9 window the daily counts match `acn_clean_sessions.csv` on 217 of 218 days; the only difference
  is the partial last day of the earlier download.
- **Missing periods.** 04 Aug 2020 – 17 Nov 2020 (106 days) has no records at all and is treated as
  missing, not as zero demand. All other zero days are real (holidays, lockdown).
- **Regimes.** Free charging → paid charging (1 Nov 2018) → COVID-19 shutdown (16 Mar 2020) → no records → recovery
  (from 18 Nov 2020). Changepoint detection on weekly volume finds the paid-charging, shutdown and recovery
  breaks within 18 days of these dates (the paid-charging break lags because drivers adjusted over November).
  Each regime is analysed and modelled separately.
- **Campus closures.** Birthday of Martin Luther King, Jr., Christmas Day, Day after Thanksgiving, Independence Day, Labor Day, Memorial Day, New Year's Day, Thanksgiving Day, Washington's Birthday, Winter break (24-31 Dec) reduce demand to well under 60% of a normal day; Columbus Day, Juneteenth National Independence Day, Veterans Day do not, so they are
  treated as normal days.

## Time-based patterns

- **Trend.** Typical weekday demand was 80 sessions under free charging and 39 under paid charging
  (-51%); it fell by 96% in the shutdown, and in the recovery reached 22 sessions per
  typical weekday in Aug 2021, growing 3.6% per week since Mar 2021. Inside the paid
  regime the level declined slowly (-4.6 sessions per typical weekday per year, 2019 onwards).
- **Daily pattern.** Weekday arrivals peak at 8:00 (paid regime; 49% of arrivals between 06:00 and
  10:00). Because most vehicles stay for hours, the number plugged in peaks later: around 14:00 (paid) and 11:00
  (free charging).
- **Weekly pattern.** A weekend day carries 56% (free), 30% (paid) and 64% (recovery) of a
  typical weekday's demand. Busiest weekday: Tue (free), Tue (paid), Fri (recovery).
- **Peaks and capacity.** Under free charging the median weekday peak occupied 80% of stations and 46%
  of weekdays reached 80%; under paid charging the median was 50% (0% of weekdays at 80% or more),
  and in the recovery 11%.
- **Seasonality.** The weekly cycle is the dominant seasonality (detrended lag-7 autocorrelation 0.69 paid,
  0.73 free; weekly seasonal strength 0.69 in the paid regime). Annual seasonality is weak and only
  observable in the paid regime: July–August 2019 ran -10% against the rest of 2019, and typical weekday demand
  in December–February changed by -13% on average from the first winter to the second (the first December was still
  adjusting to paid charging). With a single year of stable data, annual seasonality is not modelled.
- **Anomalies.** 48 days deviate strongly from their regime's trend and weekly pattern; 33 are campus
  closures or days next to one, 15 are unexplained. The COVID-19 shutdown is the largest anomaly of all and
  is treated as its own regime.

## Forecasting

- **Method.** SARIMAX + closures + drift: SARIMA(1, 0, 1)×(0, 1, 1)₇ with a campus-closure regressor, chosen by a 28-day rolling-origin
  backtest. Best MAE: 4.45 sessions/day in the paid regime (35% below the 8-week weekday-mean
  baseline) and 3.18 in the recovery regime (18% below). A drift term is used only where it improves
  the backtest.
- **Held-out weeks.** MAE 5.7 (WAPE 19%) for the 8 weeks before the shutdown and
  3.9 (WAPE 19%) for the last 4 weeks of data; the 80% intervals covered 86% and
  61% of days. In the backtest the recovery model's 80% interval covered 65% of days and the
  forecasts were too low by 1.5 sessions/day on average: while demand is growing, the intervals are too narrow and the forecast is conservative.
- **Forecast (14 Sep – 08 Nov 2021).** 24.7 sessions/day on average, 28.3 on typical weekdays,
  about 300 kWh/day. Busiest forecast day: Fri 05 Nov with 32 sessions (80% upper bound
  37). Highest weekday on average: Fri. No campus closure falls in the horizon.
- **Expected peak periods.** Weekday arrivals peak at 8:00; the number of vehicles plugged in stays near its daily
  maximum between 10:30–15:45. Expected peak on the busiest day: 21 vehicles (upper planning bound 32
  over the whole horizon), against 33 stations used in the last 28 days and 55 installed.

## Implications for workplace charging capacity planning

1. **Short term.** The expected peak (21 vehicles on the busiest day) fits comfortably, but the upper planning bound (32) comes close to the 33 stations in recent use (97%). No new installation is needed (55 stations exist), but stations that have been idle should be brought back into service before demand grows further. Spare stations can be scheduled for maintenance on weekends and outside 10:30–15:45.
2. **Plan for the pre-COVID level, not the current one.** Recovery demand is still growing 3.6% per week. If
   it returns to the paid-regime level (39 sessions per typical weekday), busy weekdays would need about 37 chargers at peak (95th percentile of the paid regime), more than the 33 stations in recent use but within the 55 installed. Re-run the forecast
   every few weeks and use the 95% band as the planning range, which compensates for the narrow intervals seen in the
   backtest.
3. **Pricing changed demand more than any calendar effect.** Paid charging changed typical weekday demand by
   -51% and removed the near-capacity days seen under free charging (46% of weekdays at 80% or
   more). Tariff policy is the strongest lever for managing peaks, and any policy change starts a new regime on which the
   model must be refitted.
4. **Target peak management at late morning to mid-afternoon, not at arrival time.** Arrivals bunch in the morning, but
   occupancy peaks hours later because vehicles stay plugged in long after charging; idle fees or move-your-car rules act
   on that window.
5. **Closures are predictable low-demand days.** They are known in advance and suit maintenance work. Columbus Day, Juneteenth National Independence Day, Veterans Day
   are normal working days at this site.

## Limitations

- One site (Caltech, 55 stations). No regime covers two full annual cycles, so annual seasonality is not modelled.
- The 106-day period without records ends just before the recovery regime; whether the site was closed is unknown.
- The data end while the recovery is still growing (September 2021), the most uncertain point to forecast from.
- Peak occupancy is derived from the session forecast through a fitted relation (R² = 0.94), not forecast directly.
- The notebooks for Issues #9–#15 still use the partial Apr–Nov 2018 download; re-running Issue #9 on the full
  collection would align them with this analysis. Detailed forecast evaluation belongs to Issue #17.
